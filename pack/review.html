Kingmaker Sharpening review
Six adversarial lenses · 2026-08-31

Where the concept must get sharper, and where it must be said sharper.

Six persona reviewers (a CRO buyer, a cynical Principal SE, a 25-year search veteran, a brand strategist, a conversion copywriter, a skeptical investor) read the live site, the blueprint, the pricing terms, the Charter, and the outreach playbook, then a synthesis ruled on their conflicts. Verdict: the concept is sharper than the average funded category play. What is dull is the seam between documents, and the gap between claims and inspectable proof. Almost every fix required is already written somewhere internally; it needs finishing, not inventing.

The panel

CRO buyer 3 critical · 5 important

Understands the offer in ten seconds and finds the 1+1 card attractively simple, provided the site says why the price is low. What stops the email: no process after the click, no confidentiality promise on the org score, and one stat-theater line a skeptic can catch.

Principal SE 2 critical · 5 important

Clears the bar almost no recruitment page clears: the manifesto is true about the job and the scorecard proves lived presales knowledge. Wants the Charter itself, the named agent, and the specimen approach message before sending anything.

Search veteran 2 critical · 7 important

Best candidate-side architecture seen at this scale, but two structural faults a veteran client finds in a fortnight: free-to-abandon exclusivity, and a surprise second invoice six months after anyone thought about Kingmaker.

Brand strategist 2 critical · 8 important

The naming spine (Kingmaker, Register, Standard, Report, Fellow) is 90 percent built and better than most funded plays. The kingmaker metaphor is dropped after the hero, and the page's one urgency device is shapeless.

Conversion 3 critical · 6 important

Persuades like an institution, converts like a brochure: every path ends at a bare mailto for a €25-40k decision, with the differentiated process invisible and zero honest proof density on the page.

Investor 3 critical · 5 important

The most honestly self-documented recruiting venture reviewed. Three gaps: the partnership exists only on the site, leadership pricing donates the scarcest founder hours, and the data moat is a slogan until counts are published.

The three moves that matter most

Today, before any dial

Purge the dead v1 rate card from the pitch machinery and fix the two site lines that contradicted the 1+1 card ("sharper terms", "replacement guarantee"). A CRO who read the site and then heard v1 terms on the first call would end the conversation. Done: applied and verified during this review.

This month

Put money and names where the claims are: a €5,000 commencement fee credited against the first month (prices abandonment, converts exclusivity from favor to purchase), three named jurors gating all Fellow language, and the partnership written down with Daniel owning the client side.

Next sprint

Surface what already exists: the six-step search process, the signable Charter with its fee-forfeit verbatim, the named founding agent, the specimen approach message, honest live counts, and the three sentences currently internal-only (why the price is low, that the score is confidential, what the structured hour trades).

Concept: where the model itself must sharpen

1

Purge the v1 rate card everywhere

all six lenses

The outreach playbook still qualified and pitched on "20-22% of base, fee at accepted shortlist" and argued against the exact payment trigger the site promises; the contract skeleton carried ghost clauses ("engagement fee non-refundable"). Two prices in one pipeline reads as improvisation.

StatusRepaired during this review: outreach economics row, objection blocks, agenda, demand-list terms, and clause 6.1.12 all rewritten to 1+1; a repo-wide search finds zero v1 remnants.

2

Price abandonment: €5,000 opens the search

veteran · investor · CRO

Under pure 1+1 a client can sign "exclusive", take the shortlist, and abandon at week six owing €0, against a self-modeled 40% non-completion rate and a cash curve where the first euro arrives months after signature. Free-to-breach exclusivity is contingency in retained clothing.

Sharper"€5,000 opens the search, credited in full against the first month." One sentence, invisible against a €24k+ fee, filters the never-serious, pulls first cash forward four months. Then own the remaining risk reversal loudly: "Beyond the €5,000 that opens the search, nothing is invoiced before your hire's start date."

3

Hard-gate all jury language on named jurors

CRO · SE · veteran · investor

The site sells "awarded by an independent jury of proven SE leaders" in the present tense while the jury does not exist, violating the blueprint's own cold-start rule. Ten minutes of diligence finds two LinkedIn profiles and an unfalsifiable register.

SharperThe Fellow tier publishes only when three or more jurors are named on the page. Until then: Represented alone, plus one dated, falsifiable line: "The first Fellows will be named by a jury of working SE leaders now being seated. Juror names publish before the first award does." Juror terms in writing: fixed honorarium, no equity, 24-month term, recusal rules.

4

Write the partnership down

investor

Daniel exists on the site and nowhere else: the blueprint says "founder" in the singular, the outreach doc says "one owner (Jacob)", every template signs Jacob. The 15-year search professional owns nothing on paper while the winnable first wave is Stockholm-heavy.

SharperA blueprint section of five sentences: Daniel full-time from a date, owning the client side (named accounts, diagnostics, search execution) and Stockholm; Jacob owning the candidate side (Register, vetting, content, Jury) and Copenhagen; one named owner per document; both holding 12+ months runway. Re-voice the company-side scripts as Daniel's.

5

Leadership at 1.5 + 1.5, with 1+1 as the endorsed-employer privilege

investor · CRO

A US VP search at 1+1 bills about $70k where the market pays $126-139k, on searches that cost 2-3x the hours, in the year cash is thinnest. And the retained option is gated on references, so it cannot even be offered when a client asks for a classic structure.

SharperLeadership default: "a month and a half when they start, a month and a half at six months" (about 25% of OTE, same one-sentence form). Reserve 1+1 as the privileged leadership rate for employers scoring 80+, which gives the Standard commercial teeth on day one. Un-gate retained thirds whenever a client requests confidentiality: a payment structure is not a track-record claim.

6

Installment 2 as a standing payable, not a surprise invoice

veteran

As drafted, the second invoice arrives six months after anyone thought about Kingmaker, often right after a re-org, from a firm with no leverage. The 90% survival target depends on this mechanism holding.

SharperIssue both invoices at start date, the second dated anniversary + 14 days, defined as "earned in full at start, payable at six months, waived by credit note on voluntary resignation or cause". It sits in the client's AP system for six months; a waiver becomes a credit note against an existing invoice, the reverse of chasing a new one.

7

Two-tier the comp-data labels before someone asks

veteran · investor

The Charter says "offer-verified"; the funnel verifies documents only when it benefits the engineer. At n=100 most datapoints will be interview-attested, and one question collapses the moat's credibility.

SharperLabel every datapoint "document-verified" or "attested" and publish the split ("62 packages: 41 document-verified, 21 attested"). The engine already tracks the flag; the honesty IS the differentiation.

8

Give the Standard provenance; cut the arbiter-takes-money conflict

veteran

A score sourced from client self-report is shown to candidates as "the truth, disclosed", and the privileged rate is tied to a score Kingmaker itself assigns: the conflict Michelin exists to avoid.

SharperEvery candidate-facing scorecard carries "scored from declared data at intake; re-verified against outcomes". The 80+ mark requires three or more dimensions verified from independent evidence plus a re-score at the first placement's six-month mark. Publish the rubric and give clients a written appeal route.

9

An NDA-first path where k=5 arithmetically fails

veteran

At the very top of a small market the anonymity arithmetic fails by definition, and the coarsen-until-it-passes remedy deletes exactly the attributes that justify the fee.

SharperA distinct, logged process state: where k cannot be met at a specificity worth sending, no anonymized dossier goes out at all; the flow is NDA first, then named reveal on the engineer's explicit per-company yes. Charter voice: "Sometimes the arithmetic fails. When it does, we tell you, and nothing moves without the NDA and your yes."

10

Cap the founding cohort and price the early risk

conversion · SE · investor

"There will only ever be one founding cohort" is the page's only urgency and it is shapeless: no size, no close condition, no stated privilege. A rational elite SE waits for year two, when joining is strictly safer.

Sharper"The founding cohort closes at one hundred names. Those names, with consent, form the founding roll, hold nomination rights into the first Fellow jury pool, and see every Report first, permanently." Early risk must buy something the year-three joiner can never buy.

Communication: where it must be said sharper

1

Fill the void after the click

CRO · conversion · brand

Every path ends at a bare mailto for a €25-40k decision, while the genuinely differentiated process (same-day inventory answer, 45-minute brief-as-diagnostic, 48-hour written go/no-go) sits invisible in a playbook.

RewriteA numbered "How a search runs" strip in the engagements section: 1. Same day, an honest answer: does the Register plausibly hold your match. 2. Forty-five minutes: the brief, and your organization scored. 3. Forty-eight hours: your scores and a written go/no-go, from a founder. 4. Signed brief: shortlist in 7 days or 3-4 weeks, and you know which before you sign. 5. Start date: the first invoice. 6. Month six: the second, only if they stayed. Route the companies door there, not to the mailto.

2

Kill the lines that contradict the rate card

CRO · brand · conversion · investor

"Founding searches trade sharper terms" promised the discount the pricing doctrine bans; "a replacement guarantee" used the word the terms refuse.

StatusRepaired during this review: the apply line now reads "Founding clients keep the 1+1 rate permanently; the trade is a named case study when it works", and the search cell now sells the real mechanism: "half the fee at risk until the hire has stayed six months."

3

Publish the Charter with its teeth

SE · CRO · veteran

The site invokes the Charter everywhere and links it nowhere. The fee-forfeit clause, the single most convincing sentence in the venture, is hidden in a markdown file.

Rewrite"Read the Charter" linking the signable document, and the forfeit verbatim on the page: "If a company you did not authorize ever learns of your search through our fault: you hear about it within 24 hours, we terminate that client engagement, and we forfeit the fee. That clause is in our client contracts."

4

Say the money argument out loud

CRO · veteran · conversion · brand

16.7% with no anchor reads as "what is wrong with them?" to a buyer who pays 25-33%. The answer exists internally and is never said on the page.

RewriteAbove the rate card: "Simple on purpose: half the fee is only earned once the hire has stayed six months. The price is our own vetting, bet on itself." And print the full waiver rule in the fine print, including that dismissal for cause also waives.

5

Replace stat-theater with checkable rules

five lenses

"We decline more searches than we accept" (zero searches run) and "this is exactly why the best candidates take our calls" (zero calls) are the two claims a skeptic catches on day one, violating the blueprint's own no-stat-theater rule.

RewriteState the rule, not the ratio: "We take a search only when the Register plausibly holds the match or we can name the path to it, and we tell you which, the same day." Rename the engineers door "For the kingmakers": it self-selects harder than "top ten percent" and finally spends the name.

6

Name the agent; give Daniel checkable rows

SE · investor

"One named agent" stays an abstraction while the uncopyable fact (your agent carried the POC and made Club himself) sits five sections away. Daniel's dossier is adjectives next to Jacob's checkable rows.

RewriteCharter row 1: "One named agent, and today that agent is a founder." Daniel's card gets one checkable number per row (searches closed, longest client relationship). If no number is available, that is a vetting problem to fix, not a copy problem.

7

Show the specimen approach message

SE

The engineer's real question is "what will you actually send me?" The site shows what companies see (the dossier) but never what the engineer sees.

RewriteA second specimen card beside the dossier: "The only message we will ever send you", a redacted approach template annotated with the Charter mechanics it obeys. Ten seconds against their inbox, and it wins the diff.

8

Answer the score-exposure fear; fix the Leadership cell

CRO

"We score the employers, too" never says the score is confidential, so the CRO imagines a public league table. The Leadership cell's "opened on placements rather than promises" reads as "we do not take leadership searches yet."

RewriteTwo sentences in the Standard section: "Your score is confidential: shown only to candidates inside your own search, never published; only the 80-plus endorsed mark ever appears in public." Leadership cell: sold on the founder with fifteen years in search, not on an internal gating rule.

9

State the trade at "Request representation"

SE · conversion

The Register section swears entry is "never by signup", then the CTA is a mailto to apply@ requesting a note: an application with better typography. The structured hour is undefined; month-one value is unstated.

RewriteAt the CTA: what the hour contains (five dimensions, one real win checked), what you leave with (your package against the verified set, the signed Charter, your bar on file), and what follows (silence, until something clears it). Rename the address represent@.

10

Honest counts, and label borrowed data as what it is

SE · investor · brand · veteran

"Know the market cold" sits over an all-US public table that reads as reprinted survey data; the outreach doctrine's best device ("we have 9 verified datapoints, and we will not pretend that is a benchmark") never made it to the site.

RewriteA live line above the table: "Offer-verified packages held today: N (X document-verified, Y attested). Updated monthly." Relabel the table "The public state of the art: what we are replacing." One franchise name: The Kingmaker Report.

Already sharp. Do not dilute.

  • The 1+1 rate card as one public sentence with the €20k minimum in fine print: all six lenses called it the sharpest commercial idea in the venture.
  • The honest two-track SLA ("discretion has a clock speed"): the rare speed claim a skeptic cannot puncture.
  • The same-day honest inventory answer: the real risk-reversal for a firm with no logos; lead with it.
  • The specimen dossier and scorecard, watermarked, with the honest 77/100. Competitors will have adjectives; keep the specimens.
  • The voice discipline: "An exam is beneath you. An award is not." Zero exclamation marks. The restraint is the proof of taste.
  • The Charter's mechanism-per-promise architecture backed by the working engine: the genuinely uncopyable part. Surface more; dilute none.
  • The naming spine: Kingmaker, the Register, the Standard, the Report, Fellow, and "Represented by Kingmaker" as a spoken status line.
  • The honest-N doctrine from outreach: extend it to the site; it is the best conversion device in the stack.

Repaired during this review

The cross-document contradictions were defects of incomplete implementation, not open decisions, so they were fixed immediately and re-verified: the v1 rate card is purged from the outreach playbook, contract skeleton, and demand list; the site's "sharper terms" and "replacement guarantee" lines now match the decided model; the shortlist standard reads "three evidence dossiers" everywhere. All 9 site checks and all 48 engine tests pass; the site artifact is republished on the same URL.
